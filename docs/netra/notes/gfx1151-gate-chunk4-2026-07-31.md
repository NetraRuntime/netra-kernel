# gfx1151 routed gate/up chunk4 decode — 2026-07-31

All results in this note are for AMD Ryzen AI Max+ PRO 395, `gfx1151`.
GPU claims are measured with HIP events or rocprofv3; serving claims are measured
with streaming host wall time. Values called derived or estimated are not direct
measurements.

## Decision

Accept the two raw AMDGCN kernels:

- `mxfp4_decode_gate_chunk4_gfx1151.s`
- `mxfp4_decode_gate_chunk4_reduce_gfx1151.s`

This is the exact Qwen3.6 routed gate/up M=1 shape: eight selected experts,
N=512, K=2048. Four adjacent 32-value MX blocks are accumulated and scaled in one
compute workgroup. The partial tensor therefore contracts from `[64,8,512]` to
`[16,8,512]` FP32, and the reducer performs eight additions for each original K
half before the final add.

The raw gfx1151 launch changes from compute grid `(1,512,1)` to `(1,128,1)` at
block 128. The reducer remains grid `(2,8,1)`, block 128. Both kernels retain the
existing 40-byte, 8-byte-aligned five-pointer ABI and caller HIP stream. Their
source metadata is wave32, 34 VGPRs, 26 SGPRs, zero LDS, zero scratch and no spills;
rocprofv3 reports allocation granules of 40 VGPRs and 128 SGPRs.

The measured clean-build gfx1151 HSACO hashes are:

| Code object | SHA-256 |
|---|---|
| compute | `7ebefa5aa26bc14d8148fe7661ecb13a90b164c8792a1d58ef70018baeabad14` |
| reduce | `7a4478e57d2a7d0dafc10c08c032947be4db0075475055b8957600802dc62622` |

The derived stable workspace is 1,048,576 to 262,144 bytes per layer, a 4x
reduction. It is allocated before hot-path launch and remains graph-pool stable.

## Correctness

Changing the FP32 reduction tree is intentionally not bit-exact to block64. It
does not change MXFP4 weights, BF16 activations, or the mathematical operation.
Every candidate was checked against an explicit FP64 reference.

| gfx1151 measured fixture | Differing FP32 values / 4096 | Max candidate-vs-block64 | Baseline mean abs vs FP64 | Candidate mean abs vs FP64 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| synthetic | 1,705 | 1.526e-5 | 1.568e-6 | 1.459e-6 |
| real layer 0 gate | 893 | 1.192e-7 | 1.050e-8 | 1.018e-8 |
| real layer 10 up | 1,013 | 1.192e-7 | 1.255e-8 | 1.245e-8 |
| real layer 20 gate | 1,349 | 1.192e-7 | 1.941e-8 | 1.870e-8 |
| real layer 30 up | 1,284 | 2.384e-7 | 1.701e-8 | 1.623e-8 |

Candidate mean error is lower on all four real-checkpoint fixtures. Candidate max
error is equal or lower on three of four; layer-30 up is slightly higher
(1.565e-7 versus 1.341e-7), still at model-native FP32 scale.

Matched-seed block64 and chunk4 output hashes agree for one of five random-ID
requests. The other four cross a later greedy-token boundary because the reduction
order changes. This is not hidden as bit-exact equivalence. The candidate itself is
deterministic: three eager runs and three full-graph runs produce the same output
hash, and eager and graph match each other.

A natural chat request completed with `finish_reason=stop` and returned a coherent
one-paragraph explanation of Rayleigh scattering. Its measured gfx1151 output-text
hash is `f3ded92cae52bc4c2100be2b670cd26da94d2c6c4a3869d7654fd1dae459b4ab`.

The structural win is four times fewer compute waves and a much cheaper reducer,
not higher reported occupancy. The candidate deliberately does four times more
work per compute wave. The `WRITE_SIZE` metric unexpectedly reports zero for the
candidate despite required global stores, so it is not used as a byte-accounting
oracle. Direct dependency-stall counters are unavailable in the gfx1151 rocprofv3
metric set.

## Graph result

Full decode graph tiers 1/2/4/8/12/16 capture successfully in 3.05 seconds and
consume 0.17 GB measured graph memory. Three exact 1+128 runs are deterministic and
bit-identical to candidate eager output. Median decode is 3416.315 ms or 37.175
tok/s, **1.204% slower than eager on gfx1151**. The raw kernels are graph-safe, but
this full-graph configuration is performance-negative for batch 1 and remains
disabled by preference.
